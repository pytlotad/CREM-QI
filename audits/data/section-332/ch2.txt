Model: full CREM mechanical integration under the active --radiation-reaction model, run until EITHER of two
independent physical limits is reached, whichever comes first: the pair's periapsis reaches the Compton barrier
(193.30 fm, where classical point-particle electrodynamics stops applying), or the osculating period/light-crossing-
time ratio drops to 150 (where the Darwin/retardation approximation breaks down; this bound tracks the pair's
angular momentum, not its radius, so it can trigger above or below the Compton barrier depending on the orbit) --
or the per-event wall-clock budget is spent (then censored, not extrapolated).  External lifetime is comparison only.
The collapse-time run uses the same limits under individual Landau-Lifshitz reaction; the closed-form remainder from where it stops
to 0.005 a_pair (~1e-6 of the total) is added.
Caution: the CREM collapse time is a classical inspiral time. Para and ortho differ here only through the initial dipole
alignment, whose coupling is ~1e-5 of the Coulomb potential, so both channels yield the same collapse distribution while
their measured annihilation lifetimes differ by ~1000x.  The comparison is a scale reference, not a prediction.

The orbit is not closed: it is an inspiral, so T shrinks as a^(3/2) while the pair sinks and no single period
describes the whole run.  T is the Kepler period 2*pi*sqrt(mu*a^3/(k e^2)) of the osculating orbit.  Every trajectory is prepared as an
EXACT circular Bohr state at a_n = n^2 a_Ps (L = n hbar and E = -R/n^2 identically, so e_0 = 0); the run ends when the
PERIAPSIS reaches the Compton barrier (193.30 fm) or the period/light-crossing-time ratio drops to 150, whichever
comes first.  The revolution count is accumulated by the orbit-averaged integrator,
resolved and skipped orbits alike.
